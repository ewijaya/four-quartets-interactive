The poet turns on his own lyric: a roundabout way of putting it, in an old-fashioned poetic style, which leaves him still wrestling with words and meanings. The poetry itself does not matter. He starts again, plainly. Old age has not brought the calm and wisdom it seemed to promise. Did the quiet elders deceive us, or themselves, handing on only a formula for deceit? Their serenity looks like chosen dullness, their wisdom like knowledge of secrets that are now dead, of little use in the darkness they either peered into or turned away from. Knowledge drawn from experience, he concludes, is of limited value.
