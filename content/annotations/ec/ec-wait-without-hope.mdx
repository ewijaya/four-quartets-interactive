The soul is told to wait without hope, without love and without thought — not because these are bad, but because anything it could hope for or love now would be the wrong thing. Faith, hope and love (the three theological virtues of 1 Corinthians 13) survive only as the waiting itself.

This is the teaching of St John of the Cross, who describes the virtues emptying the soul of its own ideas of God so that God can fill it. The paradox that closes the passage — darkness as light, stillness as dancing — recalls the still point of Burnt Norton.
