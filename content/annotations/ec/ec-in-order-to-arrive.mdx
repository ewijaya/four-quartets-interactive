The paradoxes at the end of the movement follow closely a set of maxims in St John of the Cross's *Ascent of Mount Carmel*: to reach the unknown, you must travel by an unknown road; to own what you lack, you must travel the road of giving everything up. The poem admits it is repeating itself (‘You say I am repeating…’), and the repetition is part of the method: the mind is walked round the same paradox until it stops resisting it.

The closing three lines push the paradox to its limit — what you know is what you do not know — and prepare for the Good Friday lyric that follows.
