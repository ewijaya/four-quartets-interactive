A vision. If you keep your distance, on a summer midnight in that field you can hear pipe and drum and see the villagers of long ago dancing round a bonfire. The words of the Tudor writer Sir Thomas Elyot, in his old spelling, explain that the paired dance of man and woman signifies marriage and harmony. The dancers leap through the flames and circle with heavy country feet; they are the dead who now lie under the earth and feed the corn. Their dancing keeps the same time as their lives, the time of seasons and stars, milking and harvest, mating, eating and drinking, dung and death.
