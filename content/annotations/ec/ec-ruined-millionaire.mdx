The world is a hospital endowed by a ruined benefactor. The usual reading makes him Adam, who was given everything and lost it, and whose loss is the inheritance on which the hospital runs; Raymond Preston's reading of him as Adam (1946) had Eliot's approval. Helen Gardner took all three figures, surgeon, nurse and millionaire, as types of Christ, who emptied himself of his riches, and traced the millionaire to the Zeus of André Gide's *Le Prométhée mal enchaîné*, a banker who endows men with the eagles that torment them. If all goes well, we will die of the ‘absolute paternal care’ — God's providence, which ‘prevents’ us in the old sense of the word: goes before us, anticipates us, as in the Book of Common Prayer collect that asks God to prevent us in all our doings.
