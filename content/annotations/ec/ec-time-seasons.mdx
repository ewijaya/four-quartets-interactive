Helen Gardner distinguishes the kinds of time the quartet sets side by side: the solar time of the seasons and the stars; the biological time of milking, harvest and mating; the historical time of a village and its families; and the single summer day on which the vision takes place. The dance keeps them all. The list comes down from the constellations to the farmyard. In his essays on Dante (1929) and Baudelaire (1930) Eliot had distinguished human love from the mere coupling of animals; here the two are set side by side without comment, and the passage ends with eating, drinking, dung and death.
