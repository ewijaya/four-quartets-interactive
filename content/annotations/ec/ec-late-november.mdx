Like Burnt Norton II, the second movement opens with a formal lyric in short rhymed lines, here mostly four-beat couplets and triplets. The seasons are confused: spring's disturbance and summer's creatures in November, snowdrops trampled, roses caught by early snow. The disorder spreads to the heavens, where the stars fight like armies, and ends in a vision of the world whirled towards destruction by fire before the ice returns.

Eliot told a correspondent in 1940 that the lyric was a kind of serious parody of early Yeats, touched by Blake, and its opening question has the shape of Blake's in *Jerusalem*, which asks what the golden builders are doing. Then the poet steps back and dismisses it. The pattern is deliberate: a lyric in a heightened style, followed by a plainer voice that questions what the lyric has done.
