The poem opens by reversing the motto of Mary Queen of Scots, ‘In my end is my beginning’. Houses, like families, rise and fall. They are extended, demolished, restored, or give way to a field, a factory or a new road; stone and timber go into new buildings and new fires, fires into ash, and ash into earth, which is itself made of flesh and dung, bone and leaf. Echoing Ecclesiastes, the speaker lists the times for building, living and begetting, and a season when the wind bursts into an abandoned house and stir its old panelling and its tapestry with a motto no one reads.
