An arras is a wall-hanging tapestry, named after the Flemish town where the finest were woven. This one is old and torn, shaken by the wind in an empty house, and it bears a motto no one reads any more. The poem leaves the motto unstated. In 1946 Eliot's brother Henry suggested that it is the Eliot family's own, *Tace et fac* (‘Be silent and act’), and Eliot agreed: the motto was on his bookplate, and he had dedicated *The Sacred Wood* (1920) to his father with a Latin echo of it. A silent motto is a joke on itself. It also sits beside the other motto that frames the movement, the one woven into a queen's cloth of state.
