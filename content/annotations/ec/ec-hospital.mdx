The conceit is an old one. Sir Thomas Browne, in *Religio Medici* (1643), calls the world not an inn but a hospital, a place not to live but to die in; John Hayward pointed Eliot's readers to it. Bach's cantata *Es ist nichts Gesundes an meinem Leibe* (1723) says that the whole world is only a hospital. Here the hospital is endowed by the ruined millionaire, and its patients are cured only by dying.
