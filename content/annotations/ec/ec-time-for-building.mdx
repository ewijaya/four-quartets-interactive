The run of ‘a time for…’ clauses echoes the Preacher's catalogue in Ecclesiastes 3: a time to be born and a time to die, a time to break down and a time to build up. The biblical rhythm, familiar from the King James Version, gives the passage the sound of scripture, but its content is bleaker: wind breaking a loosened window, a field-mouse behind the panelling. Where Ecclesiastes sees every season as ordained, the poem so far sees only recurrence.
