‘Grimpen’ was not a dictionary word: the *OED* admitted it only in 1972, as a marshy area, citing Conan Doyle and this line. It comes from the Grimpen Mire, the bog on Dartmoor in Conan Doyle's *The Hound of the Baskervilles*, where one wrong step off the hidden path is fatal. Eliot, a devoted reader of the Holmes stories, turns the proper name into a common noun for a place with no secure foothold.

The ‘monsters’ and ‘fancy lights’ that follow belong to the same world of moor and marsh legend — will-o'-the-wisps that lure travellers astray — as well as to Dante's dark wood.
