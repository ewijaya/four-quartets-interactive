The poet speaks in his own person, ‘in the middle way’ like Dante, and looks back on twenty years — roughly 1919 to 1939, the years *l'entre deux guerres*, between the two world wars. They are also, almost exactly, the years of Eliot's career as the most influential poet and critic in English, from *Poems* (1919) and *The Waste Land* (1922) to the war.

He calls them ‘largely wasted’. The judgment is severe and deliberate: the confessional plainness is itself a refusal of the poetic manner the second movement dismissed.
