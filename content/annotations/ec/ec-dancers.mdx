If you keep your distance, the poem says, you can hear and see on a summer midnight the villagers of centuries ago dancing round a bonfire in the field: the pipe and drum of country music, couples joined hand in hand, leaping through the flames. The warning not to come too close is the warning of fairy lore — the vision will vanish if approached.

These dancers are the dead of East Coker, and the dance is the whole round of their lives: seasons, harvests, marriages, births and death. Burnt Norton's dance at the still point was abstract; this one is earthy, heavy-footed and communal. Both return in Little Gidding's image of words ‘dancing together’.
