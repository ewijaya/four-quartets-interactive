The idea comes from Eliot's own criticism. In ‘Tradition and the Individual Talent’ (1919) he argued that when a new work of art appears, the whole existing order of works is altered, however slightly, and the relations among them are readjusted. His *Dante* (1929) speaks of a moment of shock and surprise. A doctrine about poetry has become a law of experience: each new moment revalues everything we have been, so knowledge drawn from the past can only falsify the present. Ricks and McCue also cite Bergson, whose lectures Eliot attended in Paris in 1910–11.
