The second movement refused to listen to old men's wisdom; here age is given a task instead of a reward. The idea recalls Tennyson's aged Ulysses, who sets out once more past the edge of the known world, but the exploration is inward: ‘still and still moving’ — still in both senses, motionless and continuing — into a further intensity.

The voyage ends among the sea's cries and the petrel and porpoise, which is where The Dry Salvages begins. Little Gidding answers the line in its most famous passage: we shall not cease from exploration.
