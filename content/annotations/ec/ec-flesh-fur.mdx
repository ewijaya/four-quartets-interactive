The alliteration is blunt on purpose: the earth that receives the ashes is already made of the bodies and waste of whatever lived before. Ricks and McCue hear Gerard Manley Hopkins's ‘The May Magnificat’, with its run of alliterating pairs (flesh and fleece, fur and feather). Eliot, who admired Hopkins with reservations, said Hopkins came to him too late to be an influence. This is the quartet of earth, and the line prepares for the dancers of the third paragraph, who are now under the earth and feeding the corn.
