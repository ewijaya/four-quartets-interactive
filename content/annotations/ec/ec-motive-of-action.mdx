In the soliloquy ‘To be, or not to be’, Hamlet says that thought makes great enterprises turn awry and lose the name of action. Here, in the dark of death and of the war's first winter, the senses grow cold and the very reason for acting is lost. What right action is becomes the central question of *The Dry Salvages*, where Krishna teaches action without thought of its fruits.
