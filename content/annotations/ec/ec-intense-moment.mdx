The poem sets aside the isolated moment of vision, like the rose-garden of *Burnt Norton*, for a whole lifetime burning in every moment, and more than one lifetime. Ricks and McCue trace the idea of a moment cut off from before and after to F. H. Bradley's *Ethical Studies* and to Edwyn Hoskyns's translation (1933) of Karl Barth's commentary on Romans, which speaks of a Moment without before or after. As early as 1917 Eliot had told his mother that he felt time to be all at once rather than a sequence. The evening with the photograph album is the domestic version: the dead and the living together on one page.
