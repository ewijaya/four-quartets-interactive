Eliot's lecture *The Music of Poetry* (1942) describes the search, over the previous twenty years, for a modern colloquial idiom for verse: the same twenty years this passage calls largely wasted. The sense that mastery arrives too late, only for what one no longer needs to say, is the poem's own experience: the second movement has just thrown out a lyric as an exercise in a worn-out manner.
