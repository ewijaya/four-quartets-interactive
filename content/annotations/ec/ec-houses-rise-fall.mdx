‘Houses’ carries both of its senses: buildings, and families or dynasties (as in the House of Stuart). A house may be extended, rebuilt or cleared to make way for a field, a factory or a road; a family line may flourish or die out. The list moves from the timeless (stone, timber, fire) to the modern and faintly comic ‘by-pass’, the arterial roads being built around English towns in the 1930s.

The catalogue of matter that follows — ash, earth, flesh, fur, faeces, bone, cornstalk, leaf — is a single cycle: everything is made from what something else was.
