An extended conceit in the manner of the metaphysical poets, whom Eliot's essays had championed. The wounded surgeon is Christ, whose own wounds heal: his knife probes the diseased part, and through the bleeding hands (the surgeon's, and the crucified hands) we feel the healer's ‘sharp compassion’. The dying nurse is usually read as the Church, whose care is to remind us of Adam's curse, and the ‘fever chart’ is the record of the soul's sickness.

The logic is that of the whole quartet: the cure lies not in escape from the illness but through it — ‘our sickness must grow worse’.
