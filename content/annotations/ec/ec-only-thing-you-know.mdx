The riddling close recalls Socrates, who, as Diogenes Laertius reports, said that he knew nothing except the fact of his own ignorance. The last three lines turn St John's maxims into paradoxes of knowledge, ownership and place: to be dispossessed is the only true possession, and the soul arrives only by giving up the place where it thinks it stands. The same logic ends the sequence, in *Little Gidding*, where arriving back at the start means knowing it for the first time.
