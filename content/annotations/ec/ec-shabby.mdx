The adjective first read ‘worn-out’. Eliot changed it to ‘shabby’ because ‘worn-out’ had already been used in the second movement, of the old poetical fashion he dismisses there. The equipment is the poet's stock of words and techniques, which wear out with use; the military language of the passage (raid, squads, conquer) makes it the kit of a badly supplied army.
