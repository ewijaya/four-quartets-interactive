A strict lyric in the manner of the seventeenth-century religious poets, written for Good Friday. The surgeon, himself wounded, probes the diseased part, and behind his bleeding hands we feel compassion that solves the riddle of our fever. Health lies in accepting the disease, if we do as the dying nurse tells us; her care is not to comfort but to remind us of the curse we share with Adam, and that we must grow worse before we are cured. Most readers take the surgeon as Christ and the nurse as the Church.
