The opening of Dante's *Inferno*: in the middle of the journey of our life, the poet finds himself in a dark wood, the straight way lost. Eliot takes the image and extends it — not only in the middle of the way but all the way. For Dante the dark wood is a crisis at midlife, the prelude to a journey out; here it is the permanent condition of the traveller, who never reaches secure ground.

The phrase ‘the middle way’ comes back at the opening of the fifth movement, where the poet takes stock of his own life at its midpoint.
