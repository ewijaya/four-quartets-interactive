The poet anticipates the charge of repeating himself: the lines just before echo the garden, the lightning and the laughter of *Burnt Norton*. Eliot told Montgomery Belgion in 1940 that he meant repetition only at this point, where the imagery recalls the earlier poem. In ‘Poets' Borrowings’ (1928) he had argued that every poet returns again and again to a few impressions that matter most to him, looking for their final expression.

Two lines later the question ‘again?’ had a small history. On a late typescript it became ‘here and now?’ and was printed so twice, until Frank Morley at Faber objected in April 1940; ‘again’ was restored for the Faber pamphlet. The words ‘here and now’ come in the fifth movement instead.
