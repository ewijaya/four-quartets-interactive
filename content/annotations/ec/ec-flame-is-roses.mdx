The fever's chill and burning become purgatorial fire, the fire that purifies rather than destroys, whose flame is roses and whose smoke is briars. It is the first time in the sequence that fire and rose are joined in a single image. The union recurs in Little Gidding, where the refining fire of Dante's Purgatory returns and the last line makes fire and rose one.
