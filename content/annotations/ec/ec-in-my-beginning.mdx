The poem opens by inverting a motto associated with Mary, Queen of Scots: *En ma fin est mon commencement* — ‘In my end is my beginning’. In Mary's mouth the motto is a Christian hope: death is the start of true life. Turned round, it becomes something colder: whatever begins already carries its end inside it, as the houses and families of the next lines rise only to fall.

The inverted form returns at the start of the second paragraph and once more in the last line of the movement. Only in the very last words of the poem is the motto restored to its original order, and the whole quartet can be read as the work of getting from one form to the other.
