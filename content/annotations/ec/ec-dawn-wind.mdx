At dawn the vision fades and the scene opens out to the sea, where the wind ruffles the surface. Dante's *Purgatorio* begins with such a dawn: as the morning breeze drives the dark before it, the poet sees far off the trembling of the sea. Milton's Flood wrinkles the face of the deluge, and Tennyson's eagle looks down on the wrinkled sea. The dawn wind blows again in *Little Gidding* II, where the city's dawn wind carries the ghost towards the speaker.
