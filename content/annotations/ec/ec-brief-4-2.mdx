The whole earth is a hospital, endowed by a ruined millionaire, where, if all goes well, we will die of a fatherly care that never leaves us but goes before us everywhere. Death's chill rises from the feet, fever sings in the nerves, and warmth can come only through freezing in the fires of purgatory, whose flame is roses and whose smoke is briars. Our only food and drink is flesh and blood, and though we like to think ourselves sound and solid, we still call the day of Christ's death good.
