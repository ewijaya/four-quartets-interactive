A deserted house, with the wind at a loose pane and a mouse behind the panelling, is an old picture of desolation. In Tennyson's ‘Mariana’ the mouse shrieks behind the mouldering wainscot of the moated grange where Mariana waits for a lover who never comes; Byron's *Don Juan* has a mouse behind the arras. Eliot brings the mouse back in *Little Gidding* II, where dust breathed in was once a house, with its wall, its wainscot and its mouse.
