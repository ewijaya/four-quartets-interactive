East Coker is a village in south Somerset, a few miles south-west of Yeovil. Eliot's ancestor Andrew Eliot left it for Massachusetts in the later seventeenth century, and the family became one of the established households of New England. Eliot visited the village in June 1936, when he found it delightful and strangely haunted, and again in August 1937, when he walked from East to West Coker in great heat.

The poem turns the visit into a meditation on origins: a family, a village and a whole way of life seen as one turn of a cycle of building and decay. The circle closed in fact as well as in verse. After Eliot's death in 1965 his ashes were placed in St Michael's church, East Coker, where a memorial tablet carries the poem's opening and closing words.
