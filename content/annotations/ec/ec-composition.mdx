East Coker was written in the winter of 1939–40, during the months of the ‘phoney war’, and first published in the *New English Weekly* at Easter 1940; Faber issued it as a pamphlet later that year. It found a wide readership at once: a long religious meditation that spoke to the first winter of the war.

It is the poem that turned Burnt Norton into the first of a series. Eliot kept Burnt Norton's five-movement pattern almost exactly — an opening meditation, a lyric followed by a colloquial reconsideration, a descent into darkness, a short lyric and a closing reflection on words — and from here the idea of four poems, one for each element, took shape. East Coker is the earth quartet.
