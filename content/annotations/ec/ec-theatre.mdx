The first of three images of waiting in the dark. The house lights go out so that the scenery can be changed, the flats rumble in the wings, and we know the painted hills and trees are being rolled away. In 1939–40 the picture had a wartime edge: blackout instructions had been issued in the summer of 1939, and Ricks and McCue recall Sir Edward Grey's remark of August 1914 that the lamps were going out all over Europe. They also note Shakespeare's *Richard II*, where the crowd watches the new king as in a theatre, and Poe's ‘The Conqueror Worm’, in which the lights go out over the stage of life.
