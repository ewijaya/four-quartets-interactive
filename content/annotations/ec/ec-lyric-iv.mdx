Five stanzas of five lines, each rhymed a b a b b. The lines lengthen as each stanza goes on — three of four beats, one of five, and a closing six-beat alexandrine — so that every stanza ends with a slowed, weighted close. The strict form holds a sequence of harsh paradoxes: health is disease, the nurse is dying, the hospital's founder is ruined, to be warmed one must freeze.

It is the most doctrinal of the fourth movements — a meditation on the Passion in the manner of a seventeenth-century devotional poem — and it ends on the day it has been describing all along: the Friday we call good.
