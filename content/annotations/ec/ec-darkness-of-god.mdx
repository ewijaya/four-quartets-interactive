‘I said to my soul, be still’ has the sound of the Psalms (‘Be still, and know that I am God’). What follows belongs to the tradition of St John of the Cross, for whom the soul approaches God through a ‘dark night’ in which the senses and then the spirit are deprived of every support. The darkness is not an absence of God but the form God's presence takes to a soul not yet able to bear light.

The three similes that follow — a theatre going dark between scenes, a stalled Underground train, a mind under anaesthetic — are strikingly modern and everyday, as if the mystic's night could be entered anywhere.
