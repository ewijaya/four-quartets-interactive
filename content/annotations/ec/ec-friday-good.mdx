The last stanza turns to the Eucharist: blood as our only drink, flesh as our only food, the language of Christ's words in John's Gospel taken with shocking literalness. In spite of that, we like to think ourselves healthy; and in spite of that again — or because of it — we call the day of the Crucifixion ‘good’.

The paradox is in the name itself. The poem was first published at Easter 1940, which gives the ending a sharper point.
