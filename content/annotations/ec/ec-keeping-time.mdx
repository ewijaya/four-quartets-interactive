The phrase turns on its two meanings. The dancers keep time as musicians do, moving to the beat; they also keep the time of the natural year — seasons, constellations, milking, harvest, mating. The widening list sets human life inside the cycles of stars and animals and ends abruptly and flatly: eating, drinking, dung, death.

Set against Burnt Norton, this is time without transcendence: the rhythm of a world that renews itself by dying. The rest of the quartet looks for a way out of the circle, not by escaping time but by finding it transfigured.
