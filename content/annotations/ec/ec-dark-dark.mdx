The cry is borrowed from Milton's *Samson Agonistes* (1671), where the blind, captive Samson laments his darkness in the middle of the day. Milton himself was blind when he wrote it. Eliot turns a single man's lament into a universal procession: everyone, great and small, goes into the dark.

The movement returns to the descent of Burnt Norton III, but where Burnt Norton found the London Underground merely empty, East Coker finds in the darkness something to wait in — a darkness that may be God's.
