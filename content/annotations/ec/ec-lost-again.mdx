The fight is never won once and for all. Writing on the philosopher F. H. Bradley in 1927, Eliot said there are no lost causes because there are no gained causes; in ‘That Poetry is Made with Words’ (1939) he wrote of the poet's need to win back what earlier poets knew. Written in the first winter of the war, the lines also recall the trench warfare of 1914–18, with its attacks and counter-attacks over the same few yards. *Little Gidding* III returns to the thought: we inherit from the defeated what they could leave us.
