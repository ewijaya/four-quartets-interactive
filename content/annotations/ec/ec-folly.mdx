Instead of the wisdom of old men, the poet asks to hear about their folly. When the poem was translated into French, Eliot noted that he meant foolishness, not madness. The line looks towards Yeats, who in ‘An Acre of Grass’ (1938) prays for an old man's frenzy, and whose honesty about old age Eliot praised in his first Yeats lecture, in Dublin in 1940. The ghost of *Little Gidding* II lists the gifts reserved for age, and they are much bleaker.
