Eliot told a correspondent in 1951 that the lyric had been influenced by the rosary, and especially by the Sorrowful Mysteries, the five meditations on Christ's Passion: the agony in the garden, the scourging, the crowning with thorns, the carrying of the cross and the crucifixion. Behind the wounded healer lies Isaiah's suffering servant, with whose stripes we are healed, and a long tradition of preaching on Christ the physician; Lancelot Andrewes, whom Eliot revered, spoke in a Christmas sermon of the physician slain, whose flesh and blood are made into our medicine. The poem was published on 21 March 1940, the day before Good Friday.
