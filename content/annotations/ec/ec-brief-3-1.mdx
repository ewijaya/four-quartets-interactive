Echoing the blind Samson's cry in Milton, the movement opens with a roll-call of the powerful and respectable: captains, bankers, men of letters, patrons of the arts, statesmen, civil servants, chairmen, industrialists and contractors. All of them go into the dark of death, along with the reference books that recorded their standing. Sun and moon go dark, sense grows cold, and the reason for acting is lost. We all go with them, to a silent funeral where there is no one to bury. Eliot noted that this darkness is not the mystic's darkness.
