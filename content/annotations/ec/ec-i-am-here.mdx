The speaker withdraws from the scene into uncertainty about where he is. Ricks and McCue point to a sermon in which Donne confesses that he is never all present: as he preaches, he is also at home in his library weighing what the Church Fathers said of his text, and wondering what his hearers will say to one another afterwards. Eliot quoted the passage in his essay on Lancelot Andrewes (1926), to contrast Donne's restless mind with Andrewes's absorption in his subject. The phrase comes back in the fifth movement: here or there does not matter.
