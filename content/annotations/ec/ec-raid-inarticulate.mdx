The language of writing becomes the language of war: a raid, shabby equipment, undisciplined squads, a fight to conquer and recover lost ground. Written in 1940, the metaphor is not decorative. Every attempt to say something is a new start, and each fails in its own way, because by the time one has mastered the words, one no longer has that thing to say.

What there is to win has already been won, by greater writers; the only task is to recover it, ‘under conditions / That seem unpropitious’. Burnt Norton complained that words strain and crack; East Coker makes the struggle a campaign.
