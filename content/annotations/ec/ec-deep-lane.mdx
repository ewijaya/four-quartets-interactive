The lanes into East Coker are sunken between high banks and hedges, so that on a summer afternoon they are dark under the branches even when the fields are in full sun. The passage follows a walker into the village: stepping aside against the bank for a passing van, then carried on by the lane itself, which ‘insists’ on its direction.

The details are exact and ordinary — the heat, the haze, grey stone that swallows the light, dahlias asleep in a garden — and the effect is of a trance. On his visit in 1937 Eliot photographed the road from Yeovil to East Coker, and in 1940 he sent the photographs to John Hayward. Everything is ready for a vision, and the next paragraph supplies one.
