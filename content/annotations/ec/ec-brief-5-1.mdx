The poet speaks for himself: in the middle of life, after twenty years between the wars spent learning to use words. Each attempt is a new start and a new kind of failure, because by the time he has mastered words for something he no longer needs to say it. Each poem is a raid on the inarticulate, made with worn equipment and emotions like undrilled recruits. What is to be won has already been found by greater poets, and it is not a competition: there is only the struggle to win back what keeps being lost, in times that seem unpromising. Our part is simply to try; the outcome is not our concern.
