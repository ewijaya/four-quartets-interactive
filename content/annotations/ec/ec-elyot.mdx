The lines in old spelling come from *The Boke Named the Governour* (1531) by Sir Thomas Elyot, a Tudor treatise on the education of those who will govern. In his chapters on dancing, Elyot explains that a man and a woman dancing together signify matrimony, and that their joined hands betoken concord.

Eliot quotes, with small changes, in the original orthography. The Tudor spelling — *daunsinge*, *coniunction*, *eche* — works like a sound from the past breaking into the present, and it makes the rustic dance a sacrament of order as well as a festival.
