The movement turns from the Underground's half-light to a true darkness, and the imperative changes the tone from satire to spiritual direction. The language of descent into solitude, privation and emptiness is the language of the sixteenth-century Spanish mystic St John of the Cross, who taught that the soul approaches God through a ‘dark night’ in which it is stripped of everything it holds on to.

This is the first appearance of the via negativa — the way of negation — that East Coker III will develop at length.
