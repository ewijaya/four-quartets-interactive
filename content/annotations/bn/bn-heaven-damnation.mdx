A striking claim: our entanglement in time shields us from both heaven and damnation, neither of which flesh could endure. It restates, in theological terms, the first movement's observation that human beings cannot bear very much reality. The ultimate realities are as dangerous as they are desirable. In 1936 Eliot pointed a correspondent to a sermon of Newman's arguing that heaven itself would be a kind of hell to an irreligious man: the fear of hell has its counterpart in a fear of heaven.
