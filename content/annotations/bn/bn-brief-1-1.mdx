The poem opens as philosophy, not scene. If past and future are both somehow contained in the present, and all time is always present, then nothing that has happened can be undone or ‘redeemed’. What might have been, the life we did not live, is only an abstraction, a possibility that stays forever possible and never becomes fact. Yet both the actual past and the imagined one lead to the same place: the present moment, which is the only time we have.

The lines began as a speech for a priest in *Murder in the Cathedral*, cut before the first performance.
