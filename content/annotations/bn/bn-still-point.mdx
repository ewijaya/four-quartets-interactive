The governing image of the whole sequence: the point at the centre of a turning wheel that does not itself move, around which all movement is ordered. It is neither motion nor fixity, neither past nor future — the place where ‘the dance is’.

Eliot had used the phrase before, in ‘Triumphal March’ (1931), the first part of the unfinished *Coriolan*. Readers have linked the image to Aristotle's unmoved mover, to the Buddhist and Taoist figure of the hub of a wheel, and to the single point of light on which, in Dante's *Paradiso* XXVIII, ‘heaven and all nature depend’. The poem does not choose between these; it presents the still point as something known only in fleeting moments of experience and then named with difficulty.
