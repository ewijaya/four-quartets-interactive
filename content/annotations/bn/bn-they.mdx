The poem never says who ‘they’ are. Readers have suggested the ghosts of the house's past, the walkers' own former or unrealised selves, or the children who were never born. The indeterminacy is the point: presences felt rather than seen, moving in a formal pattern as if in a dance.

Rudyard Kipling's story ‘They’ (1904), in which a visitor to an English country house senses the ghosts of dead children in its garden, is one of the sources Eliot himself acknowledged, in a letter to John Hayward in 1941; he admired Kipling and that year edited a selection of his verse. Asked about the word in 1946, he said that ‘they’ has no antecedent: the presences are people of former times.
