Burnt Norton was not planned as the first of four poems. Eliot later said that it began with passages that had to be cut from his verse play *Murder in the Cathedral* (1935), and it was first published as the last poem in *Collected Poems 1909–1935* (1936), as a free-standing work.

Only in 1940, with East Coker, did the idea of a set of poems with a common pattern take shape; The Dry Salvages (1941) and Little Gidding (1942) followed during the war, and the four were first published together as *Four Quartets* in New York in 1943 and London in 1944. Burnt Norton therefore sets a pattern the later poems choose to follow, rather than one it was written to fit.
