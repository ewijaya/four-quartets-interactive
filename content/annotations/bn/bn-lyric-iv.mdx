Each quartet's fourth movement is its shortest — a compressed lyric that works like the slow movement of a string quartet. Burnt Norton's is only ten lines: short, irregular lines, delicate rhyme, and a single question hanging over the grave-like dark (the yew, the chill, the clematis). It resolves, briefly, in the kingfisher's flash of light answering light.

Compare the later fourth movements: East Coker's hymn of the wounded surgeon, the prayer to the Lady in The Dry Salvages, and the dove of Little Gidding.
