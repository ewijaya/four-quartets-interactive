The same sentence is spoken by Archbishop Thomas Becket in Eliot's play *Murder in the Cathedral* (1935), shortly before his death — one of the passages that link the play to Burnt Norton, which grew in part from material cut from it.

In the play the reality is martyrdom; here it is the moment in the garden. In both, human beings are protected — and limited — by their inability to sustain intense experience. Burnt Norton II returns to the thought, making the weakness of the body a protection from heaven and damnation alike.
