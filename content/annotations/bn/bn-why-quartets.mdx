The collective title invites a musical reading. Each quartet has five movements; themes are stated, developed, varied and recalled across them; and the fourth movement is always a short lyric, like a slow movement. In ‘The Music of Poetry’ (1942) Eliot suggested that the use of recurrent themes is as natural to poetry as to music, and that a poem might be organised by something like contrasting movements.

Eliot is known to have admired Beethoven's late string quartets, in particular the A minor quartet, Op. 132. The analogy should not be pressed too hard — these are poems for one voice — but it explains why the sequence rewards reading the four quartets side by side, movement by movement.
