The Underground's dim light is defined by what it is not: neither the daylight that gives things clear form, nor the darkness that could purify the soul. Readers have compared the passengers to the souls in the vestibule of Dante's *Inferno* (canto III), who lived without either blame or praise and belong neither to heaven nor to hell. It is the in-between state, not evil but empty, that the poem finds most desolate.
