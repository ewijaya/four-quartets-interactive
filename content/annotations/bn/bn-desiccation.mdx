Three parallel phrases name a threefold emptying: of the senses, of the imagination (‘fancy’), and of the spirit's own activity. St John of the Cross describes a ‘night of sense’ followed by a ‘night of spirit’; the poem's list follows that order, moving from the body inward. The abstract Latinate nouns — desiccation, evacuation, inoperancy — themselves feel drained of colour; the *OED* took ‘inoperancy’ into the dictionary with this line as its only example. ‘The world of sense’ recalls the title of Bertrand Russell's lecture ‘The World of Physics and the World of Sense’ (1914).
