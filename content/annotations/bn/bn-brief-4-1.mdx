A short lyric at dusk. The day is buried by time and the tolling bell, and a black cloud takes the sun. The questions that follow ask, with rising fear, whether the living things of the garden, the sunflower and the clinging clematis, will bend towards us, and whether the cold fingers of the yew, the tree of graveyards, will close over us. The answer comes as an image of light. The kingfisher's wing flashes back the light, falls silent, and the light remains, still, at the still point round which the world turns.
