The alternative is to go further down, into the real darkness that the mystics describe, especially St John of the Cross. It means solitude; a world that is not a world; inner darkness; the loss of every possession; the drying up of the senses, the emptying of the imagination, even the stilling of the spirit's own activity. This way down is the same as the way up, as Heraclitus said, and it proceeds by keeping still rather than by moving. Meanwhile the ordinary world runs on its rails of craving, through past and future.
