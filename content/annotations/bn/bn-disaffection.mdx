The third movement descends into the London Underground. The setting is never named, but the tube passengers, the wind that drives them and the list of London districts make it unmistakable. The station is usually taken to be Gloucester Road, near Eliot's lodgings in South Kensington, from which the Piccadilly line ran to Russell Square and the Faber offices in Bloomsbury; he would have known the tube at rush hour.

‘Disaffection’ means estrangement, the absence of affection or attachment. This is a place without love or purpose — not yet the purifying darkness the poem wants, but a half-light where nothing is either fully seen or fully renounced. East Coker III will return underground.
