A kingfisher's wing shows as a sudden flash of electric blue, and here it answers light with light, reflecting the sun. The lyric's dark questions are not answered in argument but in an image: a moment of brilliance, after which the light is still — both motionless and continuing — at the still point of the second movement.

Gerard Manley Hopkins's sonnet beginning ‘As kingfishers catch fire’ (published 1918) makes the bird a figure of each thing flashing out its own nature; it is a possible, not a documented, source.
