In a pattern — a jar's shape, a composed piece of music — the end is present from the start: the whole is conceived before the first note is played. The thought becomes the refrain of the next quartet, East Coker, which begins and ends with a statement about beginnings and ends, and it returns in the final movement of Little Gidding.
