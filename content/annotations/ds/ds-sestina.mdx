The lyric is built of six six-line stanzas, and each stanza reuses the same six rhyme-sounds in the same order: -ailing, -owers, -less, -age, -able, -ation. This is a variation on the sestina, the Provençal form in which six end-words recur in rotating order; Eliot keeps the order fixed and rhymes on sounds, not words, often on unstressed suffixes. The last stanza returns almost to the words of the first.

The difficulty is audible, and it suits the subject: endless repetition, drifting wreckage, the fishermen forever bailing. The form enacts the question it keeps asking — where is there an end of it?
