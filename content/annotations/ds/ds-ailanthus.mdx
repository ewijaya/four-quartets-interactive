Four images from a St Louis childhood, one for each season: a nursery bedroom, an April yard, grapes on the autumn table, a family circle under winter gaslight. The river's rhythm was present in all of them, felt rather than seen.

The ailanthus, or tree of heaven, is a fast-growing tree from China, planted widely in American cities in the nineteenth century and notorious for the strong, unpleasant smell of its flowers — hence ‘rank’. A ‘dooryard’ is an American word for the yard by a house door; the phrase recalls Whitman's elegy for Lincoln, with its lilacs blooming in a dooryard in spring.
