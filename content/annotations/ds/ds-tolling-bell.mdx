A bell buoy is rung not by a hand but by the swell: it tolls when the sea moves it, in fog or darkness, to warn ships off rocks. Its time is ‘not our time’ — older than the chronometers sailors navigate by, older than the time counted by the anxious women lying awake for fishermen who may not come home.

The passage builds as one long sentence, through the hours between midnight and dawn, until the ground swell, which ‘is and was from the beginning’ — the words echo the Gloria Patri of the liturgy — sounds the bell on a line of its own. Burnt Norton's bell buried the day; this one measures a time beyond human reckoning.
