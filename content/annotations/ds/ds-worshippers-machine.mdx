The city has stopped honouring the river because it worships something else. In the 1930s Eliot wrote repeatedly against the modern faith in machinery and gadgets as a substitute religion. The river-god is left unappeased, but it is still waiting and watching; the repeated ‘waiting’ recalls the rhythm of Clough's *Amours de Voyage*, a poem that echoes through the sequence.
