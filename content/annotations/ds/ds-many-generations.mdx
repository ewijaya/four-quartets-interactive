The meaning that revives an experience reaches beyond one life to many generations. It is the idea of Eliot's early essay ‘Tradition and the Individual Talent’ (1919), where the poet writes with the whole of the past in his bones, turned from literature to experience itself. Geoffrey Faber objected to the lecturing tone of some phrases in the draft, and Eliot cut several; the conversational ‘I have said before’ remains.
