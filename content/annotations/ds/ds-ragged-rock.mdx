The movement ends with a rock in restless water — the Dry Salvages themselves, or any such seamark. In calm seas it is only a monument; in weather fit for sailing it is a mark to steer by; but in storm or darkness it is ‘what it always was’: a danger that wrecks ships.

The image gathers up the movement's meditation on the past. Like the rock, the agony of the past is permanent, changed only by the weather in which we meet it.
