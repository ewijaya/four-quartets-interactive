The river is the Mississippi, beside which Eliot was born in St Louis in 1888. The poem treats it as a pagan god: sullen, patient, useful, untrustworthy, first a frontier, then a highway for commerce, then only a problem for engineers. Once bridged, it is forgotten by the city — but not tamed. It keeps its seasons and its floods and waits.

Eliot returned to the river in his 1950 introduction to *Huckleberry Finn*, where he writes of the Mississippi as a presence with a will of its own, the real master of Huck's journey. The ‘I’ who begins the poem — ‘I do not know much about gods’ — is unusually casual and personal for the opening of a quartet.
