The paradox prepares for Krishna in the next movement. In Hindu tradition destruction and preservation belong to different aspects of the divine — Shiva and Vishnu — and in the Bhagavad Gita Krishna, an incarnation of Vishnu, reveals himself as Time, the destroyer of worlds. The phrase also echoes Shelley's ‘Ode to the West Wind’, which calls the wind destroyer and preserver. Eliot's line holds both at once: time destroys, and in destroying it keeps; the agony of the past endures.

The river returns as the image of it, carrying its cargo of the dead and the drowned, and the ‘bitter apple’ of the Fall.
