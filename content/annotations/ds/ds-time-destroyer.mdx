The paradox prepares for Krishna in the next movement. In Hindu tradition destruction and preservation belong to different aspects of the divine — Shiva and Vishnu — and in the Bhagavad Gita Krishna, an incarnation of Vishnu, reveals himself as Time, the destroyer of worlds. Eliot's line holds both at once: time destroys, and in destroying it keeps; the agony of the past endures.

The river returns as the image of it, carrying its cargo of the dead and the drowned, and the ‘bitter apple’ of the Fall.
