A prayer to the Virgin Mary, imagined with a shrine on a headland above a fishing harbour. It asks her to pray for everyone at sea: fishermen, all who carry on honest trade by sea, and those who direct them. It asks her also to pray for the women who have watched their sons or husbands sail and never return, addressing her in Dante's words as daughter of her own son, and as Queen of Heaven. Last, it asks prayers for the drowned, lying on the shore, in the sea's mouth, or wherever the sea bell's endless angelus cannot reach them.
