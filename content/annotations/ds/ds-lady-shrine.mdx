The fourth movement is a prayer to the Virgin Mary, Star of the Sea, patron of sailors. Readers have identified the shrine with the church of Our Lady of Good Voyage in Gloucester, built by the town's Portuguese fishing community, whose façade holds a statue of the Virgin cradling a schooner between two bell-towers above the harbour. Eliot said in 1947 that he did not know of it: he had not been back to Gloucester since 1915, and simply thought that a fishing port ought to have a shrine to the Virgin at its harbour mouth. In 1961 he said he had been thinking of Notre-Dame de la Garde, high above the harbour at Marseilles.

The prayer is for all who go to sea and all who wait on shore: fishermen, merchant sailors, women whose sons and husbands did not return, and the drowned who lie beyond the reach of the sea bell.
