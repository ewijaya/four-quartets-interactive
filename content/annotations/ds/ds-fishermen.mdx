Gloucester, on Cape Ann, was for three centuries one of the great fishing ports of North America, sending schooners to the Grand Banks and Georges Bank. The cost in lives was enormous: thousands of Gloucester fishermen have been lost at sea, and their names are recorded beside the harbour, where the Fishermen's Memorial statue (1925) looks out over the water.

The stanza refuses to imagine an end to their labour. They are to be thought of as forever bailing, setting and hauling — not as making a final profitable trip.
