The line turns the poem on a hinge, and its shape recalls a line of Kipling's: the jungle is all about us. The river is within us — the time of one life, with its source and its current, carried in the body and in memory. The sea is all about us — a time vaster and older than any life, which surrounds us on every side.

The rest of the movement belongs to the sea, and its time is measured not by clocks but by the swell that rings the bell buoy. The distinction between these two kinds of time, personal and cosmic, runs through the whole quartet.
