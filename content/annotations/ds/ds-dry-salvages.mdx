The quartet carries a headnote in Eliot's own voice: the Dry Salvages are a small group of rocks with a beacon off the north-east coast of Cape Ann, Massachusetts; the name is ‘presumably’ *les trois sauvages*, and it rhymes with ‘assuages’. A ‘groaner’ is a whistling buoy.

From 1896 the Eliot family spent its summers in a house at Eastern Point, Gloucester, on Cape Ann, and the young Eliot learned to sail along that coast. The poem joins the two waters of his childhood: the Mississippi at St Louis, where he was born, and the Atlantic off New England. It is the water quartet, and the only one set in America.
