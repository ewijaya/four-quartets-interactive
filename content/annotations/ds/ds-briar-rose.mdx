Two short lines set the scene on the New England coast of Eliot's boyhood summers: salt spray on the wild roses and fog in the fir trees of Cape Ann. His short poem ‘Cape Ann’ (1935) had already named the birds of that shore. The rose appears in every quartet; here it is neither the cultivated rose of the *Burnt Norton* garden nor the symbolic rose of the ending, but a wild briar exposed to the sea.
