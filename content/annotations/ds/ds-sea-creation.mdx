The sea throws up on the beaches evidence of a time before ours: starfish, horseshoe crabs, a whale's backbone, delicate algae and anemones in the rock pools. The horseshoe crab, common on New England shores, belongs to a lineage hundreds of millions of years older than the dinosaurs — a living reminder of ‘other creation’.

Then the sea tosses up the wreckage of human work: a torn seine (a fishing net), a broken lobster-pot and oar, and the gear of drowned men from far away. Natural history and human loss lie on the same beach.
