Eliot distrusts the idea of the past as a ‘mere sequence’ or as progress — a fallacy he blames on popular notions of evolution, which let the modern mind disown the past. Against it he sets a glance over the shoulder behind recorded history, towards a ‘primitive terror’ that is still with us.

As a graduate student at Harvard Eliot had read widely in anthropology and comparative religion, and *The Waste Land* is steeped in it. Here the interest is not in myth as a key to meaning but in a fear older than civilisation, glimpsed rather than explained.
