In the Incarnation, as Christian doctrine states it, two natures, divine and human, are joined in one person: spheres of existence that should not meet are united. Ricks and McCue cite Edwyn Hoskyns's translation of Karl Barth's *The Epistle to the Romans* (1933), where two worlds meet at the point where two planes intersect, and that point is Christ. The lines are short and plain, almost like a creed. Here past and future are overcome and reconciled.
