Written in 1940, during the Battle of Britain and the first months of the Blitz, and published in the *New English Weekly* in February 1941, then as a Faber pamphlet later that year. By now the plan of four poems was settled, and The Dry Salvages follows the pattern closely: river and sea in the first movement, a formal lyric and a meditation in the second, Krishna's counsel in the third, a prayer in the fourth, and a closing movement on the ‘point of intersection of the timeless / With time’.

It has often been judged the weakest of the four, sometimes by admirers of the others; it is also the one in which Eliot speaks most directly of his American childhood.
