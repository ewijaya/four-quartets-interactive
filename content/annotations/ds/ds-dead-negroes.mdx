The line uses the vocabulary of Eliot's time, and its bluntness is deliberate: the river in flood carries away everything in its path, and the dead are listed with livestock and chicken coops. The image is historical. The Mississippi's floods repeatedly devastated the lowlands along its course; in the Great Flood of 1927, the worst in the river's recorded history, hundreds of people died and hundreds of thousands were displaced, a great many of them Black sharecroppers and their families in the Delta, some held in levee camps under armed guard.

The ‘strong brown god’ of the first movement is here seen at its most destructive, and its victims are among those the ‘dwellers in cities’ chose to forget.
