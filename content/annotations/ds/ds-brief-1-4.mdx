In fog, a bell-buoy tolls, rung by the slow ground swell. It measures a time that is not ours, older than ships' chronometers and older than the time counted by anxious women who lie awake reckoning when their men will come home, trying to unravel the past and piece it together with the future. In the hours between midnight and dawn the past seems all deception and the future has no future; before the morning watch, time both stops and never ends. And the swell that has always been there clangs the bell.
