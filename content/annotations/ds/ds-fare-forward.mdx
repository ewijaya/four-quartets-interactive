Two journeys, a train and a liner. Passengers settle to fruit and newspapers after the grief of farewell; a ship's wake widens behind. In neither case are the travellers the people who set out or those who will arrive. The advice is not ‘fare well’ — a wish for a good outcome — but ‘fare forward’: go on, without supposing that the past is over, or that the future lies ahead.

The voice that sounds at nightfall, somewhere in the rigging and the aerial (the ship's radio mast as well as its ropes), is not heard by the ear and speaks in no language; it is Krishna's counsel, spoken to modern voyagers.
