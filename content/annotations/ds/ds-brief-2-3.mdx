The verse loosens into meditation. As one grows older the past takes on a pattern, rather than being a sequence or a story of progress, a half-truth encouraged by popular ideas of evolution that let us disown the past. Moments of happiness, not comfort or success, not even an excellent dinner, but sudden illumination, are often lived without being understood; returning to their meaning brings the experience back in a new form. And that experience belongs not to one life only but to many generations, reaching back behind recorded history to a half-glimpsed primitive terror.
