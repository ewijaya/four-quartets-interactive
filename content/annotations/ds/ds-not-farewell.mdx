The closing turn from ‘fare well’ to ‘fare forward’ comes from Thomas Hodgkin's *Theodoric the Goth* (1891), which records the saying of Severinus, a saint of the fifth century: not farewell, but fare forward. Eliot remembered it as spoken by a sibyl to Alaric the Goth. John Hayward thought instead of the epilogue to Browning's *Asolando*, which ends with the cry ‘fare ever’.
