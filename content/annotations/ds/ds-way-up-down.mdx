Heraclitus' fragment, which stands in Greek as the second epigraph to Burnt Norton — the way up and the way down are one and the same — here appears in English for the first time in the sequence, and is extended to going forward and going back.

The future is imagined as a faded song, a pressed flower between the pages of a book no one has opened: regret for those not yet born. Forward and back, past and future, become the same direction.
