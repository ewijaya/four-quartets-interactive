A catalogue of sounds heard on the New England coast, each exactly named: the sea's howl and yelp, the whine of wind in rigging, the ‘rote’ — a New England word for the sound of surf breaking along a shore — the warning of a fog signal on the headland, the groaner (the whistling buoy of the headnote) and the gull. Eliot had made a similar list of the shore birds of Cape Ann in ‘Cape Ann’, the last of his ‘Landscapes’ poems.

The long, loose lines imitate the sea's rhythm: phrases swell, break and draw back.
