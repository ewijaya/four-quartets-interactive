Travellers settle into a train with fruit, magazines and letters, and once the people seeing them off have gone their grief relaxes into relief, lulled by days of steady rhythm. Fare forward, the poem tells them, but not as an escape from the past into another life. You are not the people who left, nor the people who will arrive. And on the deck of a liner, watching the wake widen behind you, you must not think that the past is finished or that the future lies ahead.
