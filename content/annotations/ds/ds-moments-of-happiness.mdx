Not well-being, fulfilment or security — ‘or even a very good dinner’, a characteristically dry aside — but ‘the sudden illumination’: the moments of Burnt Norton, in the rose-garden, the arbour, the draughty church. The poem then adds one of its most quoted lines, about having the experience but missing the meaning. Returning to the meaning brings the experience back, changed in form.

The reflection extends beyond one life to the experience of many generations, and beyond happiness to agony, which is ‘likewise permanent’.
