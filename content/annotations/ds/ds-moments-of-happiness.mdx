Not well-being, fulfilment or security — ‘or even a very good dinner’, a characteristically dry aside — but ‘the sudden illumination’: the moments of Burnt Norton, in the rose-garden, the arbour, the draughty church. The poem then adds one of its most quoted lines: we had the experience but missed the meaning. Approaching the meaning restores the experience in a different form.

The reflection extends beyond one life to the experience of many generations, and beyond happiness to agony, which is ‘likewise permanent’.
