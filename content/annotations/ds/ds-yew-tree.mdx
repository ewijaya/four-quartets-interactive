Eliot said in 1949 that the yew is both a symbol of death and, being so long-lived, a symbol of everlasting life; chiefly, old English churchyards have yews. He added that in the Middle Ages the churchyard was a kind of munitions factory, supplying the wood for longbows. The yew here is at East Coker, where Eliot's ashes were placed in the parish church of St Michael in 1965.
