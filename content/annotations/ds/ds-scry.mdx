A satirical catalogue of ways of prying into the future: talking with spirits, casting horoscopes, haruspicy (reading the entrails of sacrificed animals), scrying (gazing into a crystal), the doctrine of signatures (reading hidden meanings in outward marks), palmistry, sortilege (drawing lots), tea-leaves, playing cards, pentagrams — and, set among them, barbiturates and psychoanalysis. By the 1930s horoscopes had become a regular feature of British newspapers, hence ‘features of the press’.

All these, whether on Asian shores or in London's Edgware Road, are ways in which curiosity ‘clings’ to past and future. The saint's occupation is different in kind.
