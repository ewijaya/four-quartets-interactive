The prose-like meditation begins with a claim about age: the past comes to have a pattern, rather than being a mere sequence or a story of development. Eliot distrusted the popular belief in inevitable progress that was drawn from Darwin, and in a talk of 1942 he wrote sceptically of the Victorian hopes built on evolution. Such notions, the poem says, let people disown the past, as though it were only a set of stages outgrown. The pattern is the subject of the lines that follow.
