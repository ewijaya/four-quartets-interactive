The central teaching of the Gita: you have a right to your action, but never to its fruits; act without attachment to results. Krishna gives this counsel to a warrior who hesitates to fight against his own kin. Eliot's ‘equal mind’ a few lines earlier renders the Gita's ideal of evenness in success and failure.

It is the same letting-go that East Coker reached in its own terms, when it left everything but ‘the trying’ to others.
