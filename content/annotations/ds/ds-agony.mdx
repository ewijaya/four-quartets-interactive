Moments of agony are as lasting as moments of happiness. We see this better in the suffering of others than in our own, because our own past is covered over by what we have done since: Ricks and McCue hear Hamlet's currents that turn awry and lose the name of action. Aristotle's *Rhetoric* observes that we pity in others what we would fear for ourselves. In 1944 Eliot wrote of seeing a photograph of a friend whose face had been changed by the sufferings of the war years.
