The third movement is built on the Bhagavad Gita, the dialogue in which the god Krishna counsels the warrior Arjuna on the eve of battle. As a graduate student at Harvard (1911–13) Eliot studied Sanskrit and Indian philosophy, and in his 1929 essay on Dante he ranked the Gita next to the *Commedia* as the greatest philosophical poem he knew.

The opening is tentative — ‘I sometimes wonder’ — and conversational, and it arrives at the Gita by way of Heraclitus: the road up and the road down are one, and so are the roads forward and back.
