The New England coast of Eliot's boyhood summers: salt on the wild rose, fog in the fir trees. The poem lists the sounds a sailor learns to tell apart: the howl and the yelp of the sea, the whine of wind in the rigging, the threat and caress of a wave breaking on water, the distant roar of surf on the rocks, a warning from the headland, the groaning whistle-buoy that ships round on their way home, and the gulls. A sailor who read the passage said these lines ring true to anyone who has sailed there.
