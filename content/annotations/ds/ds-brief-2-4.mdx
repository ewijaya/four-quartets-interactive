Moments of agony are permanent too, whether or not they arose from hoping or fearing the wrong things. We see this more clearly in the suffering of others, which we share closely but cannot bury under later action as we bury our own. People change, and smile again, but the agony remains. Time destroys and preserves at once, like the flooded river carrying bodies and wreckage, or the bitterness left by the Fall. And the rock off the coast stays what it always was: in calm weather a monument, in fair weather a mark to steer by, in storms the danger it has always been.
