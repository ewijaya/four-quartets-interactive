The people remembered are those of the English Civil War, united in the very strife that divided them. The king at nightfall is Charles I at Little Gidding in 1646. The three men on the scaffold are usually taken to be the Earl of Strafford (executed 1641), Archbishop Laud (1645) and the King himself (1649), with others after them. Those who died forgotten abroad include royalists in exile; the one who ‘died blind and quiet’ is Milton, the great poet of the parliamentary side, who died in 1674.

Royalists and republicans are mourned together: the poem is taking no side in a war three centuries old.
