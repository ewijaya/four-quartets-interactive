Harry Blamires took the hour to be the interval after the last raider had gone and before the All Clear sounded, and Gardner agreed. The phrase also has literary depth. Dante's *Purgatorio* (canto IX) has the dawn that goes before the day, and in Coleridge's *Rime of the Ancient Mariner* the agony returns at an uncertain hour. *The Dry Salvages* I had its hours between midnight and dawn.
