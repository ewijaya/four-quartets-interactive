In Elizabethan and Jacobean music a consort was a group of instruments playing together: a ‘whole’ consort of one family, such as viols, or a ‘broken’ consort of mixed kinds. A consort is also a partner, as in a royal consort. Words in the right sentence are like players in an ensemble, and like couples in a dance.

The image draws together the dance motif of the whole sequence — the dance at the still point, the rustic dancers of East Coker, the refining fire where one moves ‘like a dancer’ — and makes it an image of language.
