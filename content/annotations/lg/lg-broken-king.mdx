Charles I came to Little Gidding more than once. The last visit was at night in May 1646, when, his cause lost after the defeat at Naseby, he had slipped out of besieged Oxford in disguise and was making his way north; a few days later he gave himself up to the Scottish army. He came with his chaplain, and John Ferrar led him by lantern on a very dark night to Coppingford, where he could hide; the King, says the old account, was serene and even cheerful. A window at Little Gidding later recorded that he lay hidden with the Ferrars on 2 May 1646.

The poem imagines anyone arriving — at night like the king, or by day not knowing why — and finds that it would be the same: what brings you is only a husk of the purpose you discover there.
