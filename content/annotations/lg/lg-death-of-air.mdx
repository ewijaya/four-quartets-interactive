The three stanzas follow a saying of Heraclitus, the philosopher whose fragments stand as epigraphs to *Burnt Norton*: fire lives the death of air, and air the death of fire; water lives the death of earth, and earth the death of water. Each element dies into another. Here air dies in the dust of burnt houses, earth in drought and flood, and water and fire together in the ruin of the churches. The quartet of fire begins with the deaths of all four elements.
