Little Gidding is one of many places that are also the world's end: some on a harsh coast, some beside a dark lake, others in a desert or a city. The poem does not name them. John Hayward suggested holy places of the kinds the lines describe — island monasteries such as Iona and Lindisfarne, Glendalough beside its dark lake in Ireland, the desert of the early hermits, a saint's city — but the point is their plurality. Eliot had visited Glendalough on a trip to Ireland in 1937, and later recalled St Kevin's cave there. This one is simply the nearest, in place and in time: here, now, in England.
