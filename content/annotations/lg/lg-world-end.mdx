Little Gidding is one of many places ‘which also are the world's end’: some at the sea's jaws, some over a dark lake, in a desert or a city. The poem does not name them. Commentators have suggested holy places of the kinds the lines describe — island monasteries such as Iona and Lindisfarne, Glendalough beside its dark lake in Ireland, the desert of the early hermits, a saint's city — but the point is their plurality. This one is only ‘the nearest, in place and time’: now, and in England.
