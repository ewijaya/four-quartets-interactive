The poem turns to the place. Little Gidding, in Huntingdonshire, was the home of the devout household community that Nicholas Ferrar gathered there from 1626; Eliot visited it in May 1936. Whatever route you took, the poem says, and wherever you started from, it would be the same. In May the hedges would be white again, with hawthorn blossom this time and its heavy sweetness. The journey's end would be no different whether you came by night, like King Charles I seeking shelter there after his defeat, or by day without knowing why you came: off the rough road, round behind the pig-sty, to the plain stone front added to the chapel in 1714, and the tombstone.
