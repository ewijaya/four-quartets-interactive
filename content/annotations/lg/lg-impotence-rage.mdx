Arthur Symons's ‘Prologue: In the Stalls’ (1895) has the phrase impotence of rage, chained by enchantment; Eliot had read Symons as a young man. Eliot himself pointed to Yeats's late poem ‘The Spur’, in which lust and rage attend the poet's old age. He was unhappy with the rhyme of ‘enchantment’ and ‘re-enactment’ but could find nothing better.
