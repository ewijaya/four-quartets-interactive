The speaker cries out and hears the cry as someone else's. Ricks and McCue gather several precedents: Eliot's address at Milton Academy in 1933, where he spoke to the ghost of himself at seventeen; Bergson's account of false recognition, the sense of having lived a moment before; and Shelley's *The Triumph of Life*, where the poet meets the ghost of Rousseau and asks who he is. The ghost is partly a double of the speaker.
