The ghost will not rehearse his old thoughts and theories; they have served their purpose. Last season's fruit is eaten; last year's words belong to last year's language, and next year's await another voice. The lines are about poetry as well as about age: each generation's idiom wears out, and each new poet must find the words his time needs.

For Eliot, whose own innovations of 1917–22 had become an orthodoxy, the ghost's detachment from his own ‘thoughts and theory’ is a self-portrait as well as a portrait of Yeats.
