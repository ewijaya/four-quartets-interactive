The Epistle to the Hebrews says of Abel that, being dead, he still speaks. In Yeats's play *Purgatory* (1938), a dead woman at last knows everything, being dead. Eliot said in 1962 that we cannot fully understand anyone until after their death, when their actions fall into perspective. The thought prepares for the dead master who speaks in the second movement.
