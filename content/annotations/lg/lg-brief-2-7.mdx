Since both poets cared above all for language, which drove them to purify the speech of their people (the line translates Mallarmé's sonnet on Poe) and to make the mind look back and ahead, the ghost reveals the gifts that old age keeps to crown a lifetime's work. They are bitter. First, the cold chafing of the failing senses, with no enchantment and no promise, only the tasteless fruit of shadows as body and soul begin to part. Second, the conscious helplessness of rage at the folly of mankind, and the lacerating laughter at what no longer amuses; Eliot noted Swift here. Last and worst, the pain of living over again all one has done and been, and shame at motives revealed too late, and of harm done to others in what once passed for virtue. Then the approval of fools stings and honours stain. The exasperated spirit goes from wrong to wrong unless restored by the refining fire, where it has to move in measure, like a dancer.
