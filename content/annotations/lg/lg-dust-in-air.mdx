In 1948 Eliot explained that these lines came from the Blitz: after a raid, the debris of burnt buildings hung in the air for hours, then settled as a fine white ash on the sleeves of those who had been keeping watch on the roofs. He served as an air-raid warden in Kensington early in the war, and later did fire-watching duty at Faber's offices. The place where a story ended is also where a storey of a house came down; in the raid of 29 December 1940 some five million books burned in the publishers' warehouses of Paternoster Row.
