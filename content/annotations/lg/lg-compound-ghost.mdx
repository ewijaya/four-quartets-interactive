In the smoky dawn the speaker meets ‘some dead master’, known, forgotten, half recalled — ‘both one and many’. He is ‘familiar’ in two senses: well known, and a familiar spirit. He is ‘compound’ because he is several poets at once.

The scene is modelled on Dante's meeting in Hell with his old teacher Brunetto Latini (*Inferno* XV), whose ‘brown baked features’ are scorched by the rain of fire. Readers have recognised other faces too: Yeats, who had died in January 1939; Swift, whose savage indignation colours the speech on age; Mallarmé, whose line the ghost translates. The speaker himself is doubled — he hears his own voice as another's. The ghost is, in part, the poet's own future.
