Swift defined a good style as proper words in proper places. The balanced pairs that follow, neither this nor that, echo the most admired couplets of the seventeenth century, Sir John Denham's lines on the Thames in *Cooper's Hill*: though deep, yet clear; though gentle, yet not dull. Ezra Pound's ‘A Pact’ offers a commerce between the old poet and the new. John Hayward claimed a hand in the line about the formal word.
