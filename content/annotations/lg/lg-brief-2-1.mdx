The second movement opens with three rhymed stanzas on the deaths of the elements, built on a saying of Heraclitus: fire lives the death of air, and water the death of earth. Here the elements die in the London Blitz. An old man's sleeve is grey with ash, all that burnt roses leave. Dust hanging in the air marks where a story ended, and, with a pun, where a storey of a building came down. Dust breathed in was once a house, its walls, its panelling, its mouse. When hope and despair have both died, air itself has died. Eliot recalled that after a raid the debris hung in the air for hours before it settled as a fine white ash on the sleeves of those keeping watch on the roofs.
