A clear frosty afternoon in the depth of winter, when the low sun blazes on ice and the hedgerows are white with snow instead of blossom. The poem calls it a season of its own, ‘suspended in time’ and outside the covenant of the calendar — neither budding nor fading, not part of the cycle of generation that governed East Coker.

The paradoxes accumulate — cold that is the heart's heat, a glare that is blindness, spring that is winter — and end in a question about an ‘unimaginable / Zero summer’: a summer beyond all seasons, at the absolute point where heat and cold meet.
