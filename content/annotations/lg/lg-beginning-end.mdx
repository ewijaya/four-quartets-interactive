The last movement opens by gathering the sequence's formulas about beginnings and ends: Burnt Norton's end that precedes the beginning, East Coker's motto in both of its forms. Here they are stated calmly, almost as common sense: to make an end is to make a beginning; the end is where we start from.

The rest of the movement enacts the statement, returning to the first quartet's garden and gate at the moment of ending.
