Little Gidding was begun in 1941 and gave Eliot more trouble than any of the other quartets. He set aside a first draft, was ill for part of the year, and worked on it in the intervals of fire-watching and wartime travel; it was published in the *New English Weekly* in October 1942 and as a Faber pamphlet that December. His friend John Hayward read the drafts closely, and their correspondence — printed by Helen Gardner in *The Composition of Four Quartets* — shows many lines being reworked at his prompting.

It is the fire quartet, and the war is closer to its surface than in any of the others. It also gathers up the whole sequence: its last movement recalls images from all four poems and brings them to rest.
