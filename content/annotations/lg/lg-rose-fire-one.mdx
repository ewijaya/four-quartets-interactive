The last lines draw on the close of Dante's *Paradiso*: the celestial rose in which the blessed are gathered, and the vision in which all that is scattered through the universe is bound by love into a single volume — ‘the universal form of this knot’. The tongues of Pentecostal flame are folded into a crowned knot of fire; the fire (destruction, purgation, the Spirit) and the rose (love, memory, the garden) become one.

Every major motif of the sequence converges here. The last word returns to Burnt Norton's first garden, now transfigured: the still point found not in a moment of escape but at the end of the whole journey through time.
