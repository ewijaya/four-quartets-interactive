The third movement begins as an argument. Three states that look alike are entirely different, and they grow in the same hedge: attachment to self, to things and to people; detachment from them; and, growing between the two, indifference, which resembles both as death resembles life. It lies between two lives, flowerless, like a weed between the stinging nettle and the dead-nettle, which looks like it but does not sting and does bear flowers. Eliot admitted that the image was not a happy one.
