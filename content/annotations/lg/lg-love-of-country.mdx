Edmund Burke wrote in his *Reflections on the Revolution in France* (1790) that to love the little platoon we belong to in society is the first link in the chain by which we come to love our country and mankind. Eliot's version moves the other way as well: love of country begins in our own field of action, and grows until action itself matters less, though never nothing. Written in 1941–42, the lines are part of the quartet's patriotism, and of its correction.
