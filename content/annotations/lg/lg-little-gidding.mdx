Little Gidding is a hamlet in the former county of Huntingdonshire (now in Cambridgeshire). In 1626 Nicholas Ferrar, a former London merchant and member of parliament, settled there with his mother, brother, sister and their families and founded a household devoted to prayer: they restored the ruined church, kept the hours of the Prayer Book day and night, taught the children of the district and made fine bound concordances of the Gospels. Ferrar was a friend of George Herbert, who left him the manuscript of *The Temple* to publish.

The community was dispersed in the Civil War, and the church was rebuilt in the eighteenth century. Eliot visited it in May 1936. The last quartet takes its name and its setting from the place: a winter afternoon in an out-of-the-way chapel, where prayer ‘has been valid’.
