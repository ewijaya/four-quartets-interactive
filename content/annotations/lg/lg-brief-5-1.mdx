What we call a beginning is often an end, and making an end is making a beginning. The poet describes the right sentence in a long parenthesis that does what it describes: every word at home in its place and supporting the others, neither timid nor showy, old and new words at ease together, the common word exact but not coarse, formal words precise without pedantry, the whole company dancing together. Every such phrase both ends and begins; every poem is an epitaph. And every action is a step towards death, whether on the executioner's block, in the fire, in the sea, or under a gravestone that can no longer be read. That is where we start.
