Three rhymed stanzas, each ending with a death: of air, of earth, of water and fire. An old man's sleeve is grey with ash, all that is left of the burnt roses; dust in the air marks where a house — and a story — ended. The lyric reads like a record of a bombed city, and like a vision of the end of all things.

Heraclitus wrote that fire lives the death of air, and air the death of fire; water lives the death of earth, and earth that of water. The lyric reworks this idea of the elements living each other's deaths, with one element for each quartet: air (Burnt Norton), earth (East Coker), water and fire (The Dry Salvages and Little Gidding).
