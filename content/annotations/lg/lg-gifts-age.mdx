East Coker asked whether the elders' serenity was a deception; here the ghost answers with three bitter gifts. First, the failing of the senses, with no enchantment and no promise. Second, impotent rage at human folly — the note of Swift's last years and of Yeats's late poems, which ask why an old man should not rage. Last, and worst, the pain of re-enacting everything one has done, and of seeing that what one took for virtue did harm.

Honour stains and fools' approval stings; the spirit goes from wrong to wrong — unless restored by the refining fire.
