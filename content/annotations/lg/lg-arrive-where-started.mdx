In a paper of 1914, ‘The Validity of Artificial Distinctions’, the young Eliot wrote that a true philosophy brings us back to the exact point we started from, which is the same and yet somehow not the same. F. H. Bradley, whose philosophy was the subject of Eliot's doctoral thesis, held that the end lies hidden in what is assumed at the beginning. The lines give that philosophical circle the shape of a voyage.
