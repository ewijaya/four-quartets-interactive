The dawn encounter is written as an imitation of Dante's *terza rima*, the interlocking three-line rhyme of the *Commedia*. English is poor in rhymes, so in place of rhyme Eliot alternated feminine line-endings (on an unstressed syllable: *morning*, *unending*) with masculine ones (on a stressed syllable: *night*, *tongue*). The verse is plain, measured and grave.

In ‘What Dante Means to Me’ (1950) Eliot said that this passage cost him more time and trouble than any passage of the same length he had ever written, and that he meant it as the nearest equivalent he could achieve, in style as well as content, to a canto of Dante.
