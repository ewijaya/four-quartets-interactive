W. H. Auden's *Spain* (1937), whose Faber pamphlet was the model for the pamphlets of the quartets, ends with history saying alas to the defeated but unable to help or pardon them. Eliot's lines answer it: what we inherit from the winners is taken from the losers, and what they leave is a symbol. Matthew Arnold called Oxford the home of lost causes; Eliot wrote in 1948 that there is no such thing as a lost cause, or a securely gained one.
