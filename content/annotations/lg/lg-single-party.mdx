The enemies of the Civil War have accepted ‘the constitution of silence’ — a political word for death — and are now folded in one party. What the victors inherit from the defeated is not a policy but a symbol, perfected in death. For a poet writing in 1941–42, with the country itself at war, the reconciliation of old enemies had a present meaning.
