The worst gift is to live through everything again and see one's motives for what they were. Eliot had dramatised the danger in *Murder in the Cathedral* (1935), where the last temptation offered to Thomas Becket is to do the right thing for the wrong reason. Lancelot Andrewes, in an Ash Wednesday sermon of 1619, urged his hearers to consider their base motives. Yeats, in ‘Vacillation’, is weighed down by things said and done long ago. The third movement promises what answers this shame: the purification of the motive.
