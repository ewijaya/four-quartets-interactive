At Pentecost, according to the Acts of the Apostles, the Holy Spirit descended on the apostles as tongues of fire, and they began to speak in other languages. The winter sun's glow on the ice is likened to that fire — not a wind, but a light that ‘stirs the dumb spirit’.

Pentecost is the key to the quartet's fire. It returns at the end of this movement, where what the dead communicate is ‘tongued with fire’; in the fourth movement, where the descending dove brings tongues of flame; and in the last lines, where the tongues of flame are folded into the knot of fire.
