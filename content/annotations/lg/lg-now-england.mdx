Browning's ‘Home-Thoughts, from Abroad’ longs to be in England now that April's there. Eliot's England in 1941–42 was at war, and he later called the last three quartets primarily patriotic poems, increasingly war poems. The phrase is not a boast: Little Gidding is the nearest of the world's ends only because it is here and now, where the poet happens to be.
