An English version of a line from Mallarmé's sonnet ‘Le Tombeau d'Edgar Poe’, which praises Poe for giving ‘a purer sense to the words of the tribe’ (*un sens plus pur aux mots de la tribu*). Eliot makes it the shared task of the ghost and the speaker: their concern was speech, and speech drove them to purify the common language and to ‘urge the mind to aftersight and foresight’.

The phrase names the ideal of the whole sequence's struggle with words, which the fifth movement will describe as a sentence in which every word is at home.
