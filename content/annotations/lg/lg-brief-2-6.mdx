The speaker asks the ghost to speak, though he may not understand or remember. The ghost does not want to go over his own thought and theory, which the speaker has forgotten: they have served their purpose. Let the speaker's ideas go too, and pray that others forgive them, as the ghost asks forgiveness for his, the bad and the good. What is past is used up. The words of last year belong to that year's language, and next year's words wait for another voice. Now that the way between two worlds has grown easy for the restless, wandering spirit, the ghost finds himself saying things he never expected to say, in streets he never expected to see again, having left his body on a distant shore. The line recalls Yeats, who died in the south of France in 1939 and was buried there until his body was brought home to Ireland after the war.
