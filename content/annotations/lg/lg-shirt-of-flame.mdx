Who devised the torment? Love. The ‘intolerable shirt of flame’ recalls the shirt of Nessus in Greek myth: Hercules' wife Deianira, fearing she had lost his love, sent him a shirt steeped in the centaur Nessus' blood, which she had been told was a love charm. It was poison; it clung to him and burned him, and he died on a pyre on Mount Oeta.

The myth fits the paradox exactly: a garment woven by love that consumes the wearer and cannot be taken off. We only live, only breathe, consumed by one fire or the other.
