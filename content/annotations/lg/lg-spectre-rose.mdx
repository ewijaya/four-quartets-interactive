Why remember these dead? Not to ring the bell backward — to ring a peal in reverse order, an old alarm signal. In 1940 England's church bells were silenced, to be rung only to warn of invasion, so the image had a sharp wartime sense. Nor to conjure up ‘the spectre of a Rose’: the ghost of a lost cause, perhaps the rose of the royal house, and also the title of a famous ballet, *Le Spectre de la rose* (1911), in which the spirit of a rose visits a dreaming girl.

Eliot said the capitalised Rose stood for Charles I, the Royal Martyr. Helen Gardner notes that the phrase replaced ‘the ghost of a Rose’, and that Nijinsky's famous leap in the ballet was one of the great aesthetic experiences of Eliot's early years in London; the echo of Sir Thomas Browne, who in *The Garden of Cyrus* speaks of raising up the ghost of a rose, he did not recognise until Hayward pointed it out. Nostalgia is refused. We cannot revive old factions or follow an antique drum.
