Peering at the stranger as you size up someone met at twilight, the speaker catches the look of some dead master he had known and half forgotten, one and many at once: a ghost made of several people, intimate and yet impossible to identify. In letters Eliot named Yeats, Swift, Mallarmé and Poe among its ingredients, with Dante's Brunetto and the troubadour Arnaut Daniel. The speaker plays a double part, crying out in surprise, as Dante did, in a voice that seems another's. He knows himself and yet is someone else, and the other's face is still taking shape; but the words are enough to bring recognition. Too strange to each other to misunderstand, the two walk the pavement together on a patrol of the dead.
