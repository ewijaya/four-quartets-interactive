Whoever comes, by whatever route, must put off ‘sense and notion’. The visitor is not a tourist, a scholar or a reporter — not here to verify, instruct, satisfy curiosity or carry report — but to kneel in a place where prayer has been valid. The legal word ‘valid’ is exact: prayer here has been effective, has taken hold.

Prayer is more than words in order or the sound of a voice; and the dead, who had no words for it when living, can speak of it now, in a language ‘tongued with fire’ — the Pentecostal language beyond the language of the living.
