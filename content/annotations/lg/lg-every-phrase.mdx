A description of the ideal sentence, set inside a parenthesis that is itself an example of it: every word at home, each supporting the others, neither diffident nor ostentatious; old and new at ease together; common words exact without vulgarity, formal words precise without pedantry.

After Burnt Norton's words that ‘strain, crack and sometimes break’ and East Coker's raid on the inarticulate, this is the struggle's resolution — not a victory but a state of balance. And every such sentence both ends and begins; every poem is an epitaph.
