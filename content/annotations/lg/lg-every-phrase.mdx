A description of the ideal sentence, set inside a parenthesis that is itself an example of it: every word at home, each supporting the others, neither diffident nor ostentatious; old and new at ease together; the common word exact without vulgarity, the formal word precise without pedantry.

After Burnt Norton's words that ‘strain, crack and sometimes break’ and East Coker's raid on the inarticulate, this is the struggle's resolution — not a victory but a state of balance. And every such sentence is an end and a beginning; every poem an epitaph.
