Virgil's last words to Dante, at the top of the mountain of Purgatory, are that he crowns and mitres Dante lord over himself: his will is now free and upright. The ghost's crown is ironic, a set of bitter gifts. A draft called them the final prizes, and Hayward thought of the Latin proverb that the end crowns the work.
