Meetings with the dead have a history in the poetry of war. In Wilfred Owen's ‘Strange Meeting’ (1918) a soldier in a tunnel of the underworld meets the enemy he killed; Herbert Read's ‘To a Conscript of 1940’, which Faber published that year, imagines a ghostly encounter between soldiers of two wars. The speaker of *The Waste Land* stops a man in the City crowd and hails him by name. Later the two are too strange to each other to misunderstand, a phrase Ricks and McCue also trace to Owen.
