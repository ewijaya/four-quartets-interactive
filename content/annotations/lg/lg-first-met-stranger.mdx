In *Inferno* XV the damned souls peer at Dante and Virgil as people look at one another at dusk under a new moon, screwing up their eyes like an old tailor at the eye of his needle. Eliot keeps the searching look and moves the scene from evening to the half-light before dawn. Finding the right word for that half-light gave him trouble, and he settled on John Hayward's phrase.
