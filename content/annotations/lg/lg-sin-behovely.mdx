Words from Julian of Norwich's *Revelations of Divine Love*. In the thirteenth showing Julian asks why sin was not prevented, and is told that sin is ‘behovely’ — fitting, necessary, having its place in the order of things — but that all shall be well, and all manner of thing shall be well.

Eliot took them from the 1670 edition by Serenus Cressy, which he owned in a reprint made, as it happened, in St Louis, and understood ‘behovely’ in its old sense of inevitable, not in the later sense of useful. He sets the words apart in short lines, as a voice from another age breaking in, and said he chose fourteenth-century voices so that the passage would not become sentimental about the seventeenth; they return at the end of this movement and again among the last lines of the sequence.
