Water and fire take the place of town, pasture and weed alike. They mock the sacrifice we refused to make, and will rot the damaged foundations we had neglected, of sanctuary and choir, the holiest parts of a church. Then water and fire die as well. The lines recall the night of 29 December 1940, when incendiary bombs gutted many of the City of London's churches, several of them Wren's, and the water used to fight the fires soaked whatever had not burnt.
