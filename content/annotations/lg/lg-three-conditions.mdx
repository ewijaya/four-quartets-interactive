Three states that grow in one hedgerow: attachment to self, things and persons; detachment from them; and, between them, indifference, which resembles both as death resembles life. The image is botanical. The dead-nettle is a common plant that looks like the stinging nettle and grows beside it, but has no sting and bears flowers. When John Hayward suggested ‘the live nettle and the dead’, Eliot refused: that would suggest a stinging nettle that had died. Indifference lies between the two, unflowering: it neither stings like attachment nor blooms like detachment. Eliot admitted the image was not a happy one.

Detachment is not coldness. Memory is for liberation: not less love, but love expanding beyond desire, and so freed from future and past. Love of one's country begins as attachment to one's own field of action and grows into something wider.
