Three short lines quote the fourteenth-century mystic Julian of Norwich, who in a vision heard Christ tell her that sin is necessary, but that all will be well. The poet thinks again of Little Gidding and its people, not all of them admirable, neither his close kin nor especially kind, some of rare gifts, all touched by the spirit of the place and united in the conflict that divided them. He thinks of King Charles arriving at nightfall; of the king and others who died on the scaffold, whom Hayward identified as Charles, Archbishop Laud and the Earl of Strafford; of some who died forgotten at home or in exile; and of Milton, who died blind and so quietly that no one noticed the moment.
